What do these gains mean?

Parallel form: u = Kp·e + Ki·∫e dt + Kd·de/dt. Time is measured in seconds. Integration uses the previous error over each sample interval; D uses a backward difference. D acts on error, so a sudden setpoint change can cause a kick. Numeric fields also accept signed gains outside the slider range.

The system you control

Start with physical parameters, or bring your own continuous or digital model.

Lag ≠ dead time

A 100 ms time constant reaches 63.2% of the final value after 100 ms. A 100 ms dead time starts the response 100 ms later. Use both to model a slow system with transport delay.

How is the plant sampled?

Continuous models use an exact zero-order hold at the chosen sampling rate. The input is held between samples. Dead time is rounded to the nearest whole sample; its effective value is shown above. No hidden computational delay is added.

Watch the integral term.
Without anti-windup, error keeps accumulating while the actuator is pinned at its limit. Back-calculation feeds the clipping residual into the integrator to help it recover.

          
What does “linear stability” mean here?
Pole locations and frequency responses describe the loop with clipping inactive. Saturation makes the simulation nonlinear; a bounded, clipped response does not prove that the linear system is stable. Anti-windup corrects the PID integrator, not internal states of arbitrary output filters.
